Teaching philosophy

I treat applied economics as a craft that should be taught alongside its tools. Students learn the theory that makes a research design identify something, and the data engineering that makes the design actually run — from cleaning a noisy administrative dataset to scraping a public website. The aim is to send people out able to evaluate a policy claim on their own, not to recite a model.

A good empirical economist needs to be fluent in three languages at once: theory, code, and the institutional details of the policy being studied. None of the three is optional.
