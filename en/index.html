About

Applied economist using causal inference, digital trace data and machine learning to evaluate public policies, public-sector digitalization and institutional quality.

I hold a doctoral degree in Economics from the University of Molise. My empirical work draws on difference-in-differences designs, web scraping, machine learning and text mining applied to web, social-media and administrative data.

My doctoral training was based at the University of Molise (XXXVIII cycle, 2022–2026), with a research stay at the Universitat Politècnica de València under Prof. Josep Domenech. My work has appeared in Scientific Reports and the Accounting, Auditing & Accountability Journal, and has been presented at international venues including CARMA, AISRe, SIE and the EUI Centre for a Digital Society.

Active research lines cover EU cohesion policy, digital divide and digital public services, institutional quality and governance. The unifying thread is the use of digital footprints — municipal websites, search trends, social platforms — combined with quasi-experimental methods to recover credible policy effects.

“Digital traces, when paired with credible identification strategies, turn the web into a laboratory for evaluating the public policies that shape our economies and institutions.”
