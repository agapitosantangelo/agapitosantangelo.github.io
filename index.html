About

I am an applied economist working at the intersection of causal inference and digital trace data for the evaluation of public policies. My research combines difference-in-differences designs, machine learning and text mining applied to large-scale web, social media and administrative data.

I am currently a PhD candidate in Law and Economics at the University of Molise (XXXVIII cycle, expected April 2026), with a research stay at the Universitat Politècnica de València under Prof. Josep Domenech. My work has appeared in Scientific Reports and the Accounting, Auditing & Accountability Journal, and has been presented at international venues including CARMA, AISRe, SIE and the EUI Centre for a Digital Society.

Active research lines cover EU cohesion policy, digital divide and digital public services, institutional quality and governance, and the social determinants of health and food insecurity. Across these strands the unifying thread is the use of digital footprints — web pages, search trends, social platforms — combined with quasi-experimental methods to recover credible policy effects.

“Digital traces, when paired with credible identification strategies, turn the web into a laboratory for evaluating the public policies that shape our economies and institutions.”

Research interests & methods
